Use this guide only after a manager says the current rehearsal or pilot is ready. During P6,
use samples and outcomes clearly labeled as synthetic or demo data. Actual pilot perfume testing
starts only after the P6 readiness record says `go`.

## Sign in and choose your profile

1. Open the Fragrance Rater address supplied by the manager.
2. Complete the Authentik sign-in screen. Do not share your session or use another person's
   account.
3. Confirm that the name shown in the application is the evaluator you intend to record.
4. Stop and contact the manager if the wrong evaluator, unexpected manager navigation, or another
   person's history appears.

## Record an ordinary rating

Open **My Ratings**, choose the exact fragrance version, and record the encounter. A later
encounter creates another history entry; it does not erase an earlier experience. Leave a field
blank when it was not assessed. A zero is a real response.

## Complete a blind calibration session

Open **Your calibration** and choose the assigned program. Work from the blind code on the
physical label. Before reveal, the application should not show the fragrance identity, role,
repeat relationship, holdout status, or manager mapping.

1. Smell the blotter and record only what you perceive.
2. Submit the blotter response, then lock that stage when it is complete.
3. Apply only samples included in the manager-approved skin plan.
4. Record elapsed skin observations at the actual time observed and lock the stage when finished.
5. Ask the recorder to finalize the skin plan, including an intentionally empty plan.
6. Reveal identities only when the application says every prerequisite is complete.
7. Add any post-reveal response in the separate post-reveal area.

Do not infer identities from bottle order, labels, browser history, or another participant. Do not
photograph or share the manager's mapping sheet before reveal.

## Facilitated paper sessions (six-evaluator variant)

If your manager runs the facilitated variant of the pilot, you do not sign in during sessions. The sections above
about signing in, blind calibration on screen, and skin observations do not apply to you. Instead:

1. **No logins and no phones.** Phones stay outside the room or switched off. The facilitator, who is also an
   evaluator, types your answers into the application afterward.
2. **One paper sheet per session.** You get one sheet with three sample columns. The codes printed on the sheet are
   the codes of the blotters you will smell. Write only what you perceive. Never write what a sample might be.
3. **One blotter, passed around.** In the group format the facilitator sprays one blotter at a time and it goes round
   everyone in a fixed seat order. You smell it once, write the time you first smell it, and pass it on in silence.
   Everyone in a session smells the same three samples, in the same order, and everyone's sheet shows the same codes.
   That is a known limitation of this format: do not try to match codes or guess what others are smelling, and do not
   compare. Work silently until the facilitator announces the reveal, and do not talk about any sample or score to
   anyone, in or out of the room, until then. Your scores are marked as group-blotter scores; that is a label for the
   analysis, not a judgement of your answers.
4. **Smell as soon as you are handed a blotter** and write the time you first smell it. A provisional ceiling of
   2 minutes from a blotter's spray to your first smell applies; it is confirmed in the rehearsal. If the window would
   be missed, the facilitator sprays a new blotter of the same sample and you smell that one; its spray time is the one
   written on your sheet. If you still smell late, write the time you actually smelled. Nothing is thrown away. The
   short wait between samples for clean air is a separate thing and is not your 2 minute window.
5. **A zero is a real answer; blank means "not answering".** Do not guess a mark. If you cannot smell a sample,
   circle **No** under Detected and leave the rest of that column blank. If a mark is ambiguous the facilitator enters
   it as blank with a flag.
6. **Separate conditions slip.** If you accepted the session-context consent, you also complete a short conditions
   slip alone before the first spray and hand it back face down. It is filed apart from your score sheet and linked
   only by a random record ID. Consent was recorded once with you present before session 1; you can withdraw it at
   any time.
7. **Rank at the end, then hand in the sheet.** Rank only after all three columns are initialed, then give the sheet
   to the facilitator and stay silent.
8. **Everyone finishes before the next session.** The facilitator has to enter all six sheets first, so expect a gap
   of at least three hours between sessions on the same day.

**Skin tests.** Facilitated sessions have no skin tests. For the pilot, only the facilitator does skin tests, on
separate days. If you are not the facilitator, you are skin-absent by design; that is not a missed step.

**Novices.** If you are new to fragrance, the facilitator will show you the sheet and run one practice session with
non-pilot samples first. Everyone is included. Your repeat answers are described, not graded, and no exclusion
threshold is applied.

**Your paper.** The original sheet and slip are kept and scanned by the facilitator under the retention period fixed
for the pilot. The sheet carries a version id (`facilitated-session-sheet v2.0` at the time of writing, still a
draft); a sheet with no version id is not used.

Printed forms: [session sheet](../facilitated-session-sheet.md), [conditions slip](../facilitated-conditions-slip.md).

## Review recommendations

Open **Recommendations** to create a new set or reopen a saved set. Record interest before
sampling. If you later sample a recommendation, update its sampling state and link the later
ordinary outcome when available. An explanation can help describe the deterministic match, but
it is not a probability or a promise that you will like the fragrance.

## Interruption or connectivity failure

After reconnecting, reopen the same page and verify the last saved response before submitting
again. If the application cannot be reached, use the manager's
[printed evaluation and outage form](../printable-evaluation-form.md). Record the blind code,
stage, elapsed minutes, and local time. Give the form to the assigned recorder; do not write the
concealed identity on it. The recorder will enter the data and record the operational failure
after service returns.

## Report a problem

Stop the session and contact the manager if:

- an identity or experimental role appears before reveal;
- a saved response disappears or changes;
- the application allows entry after a stage is locked;
- the wrong evaluator or program is shown;
- the page cannot be completed with the keyboard or on the assigned device;
- a severe physical reaction occurs: stop exposure immediately, seek emergency help under the
  local emergency procedure, and notify the manager once it is safe to do so.

The application records preferences, observations, and operational events. Avoid entering
passwords, medical details, or unrelated personal information in free-text fields.
